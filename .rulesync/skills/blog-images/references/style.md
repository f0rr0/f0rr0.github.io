## Stable visual grammar

| Attribute | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Medium | Layered matte cut-paper editorial collage |
| Background | Opaque warm ivory paper, subtle natural grain, near `#faf2df` |
| Paper pieces | Cream and ivory, softly irregular cut edges; discernible through shallow shadows |
| Accent | Warm amber/saffron for focal actions; allow bolder teal, coral, cobalt, or plum fields where the subject benefits |
| Supporting colour | Select two or three harmonious colours per article from sage/olive, teal, coral, cobalt, plum, and saffron; ground them with cream and charcoal |
| Depth | Several overlapping paper layers, folded edges and short soft shadows from consistent diffuse light |
| Viewpoint | Overhead, front-on, or shallow isometric stage; choose the angle that makes the action clear |
| Composition | One recognisable focal action, supported by a natural environment; overlap, diagonals and varied scale create movement |
| Mood | Warm, thoughtful, matter-of-fact; suitable for personal engineering stories |
| Typography | Sparse and earned; usually no lettering in scene art. Keep an exact token or date only when its identity contributes more than an object or gesture could |

These hex values approximate the selected artwork and guide new briefs. They are not exact measured colour guarantees or changes to the site's UI tokens. The reference governs paper character; the author's richer composition and colour request governs the new scenes. Preserve gentle paper texture without making it dirty, distressed, or visually noisy. Avoid ink outlines, graphite hatching, glossy clay, photorealistic objects, deep theatrical shadows, neon, and generic robot mascots.

Keep important objects and labels clear of export edges, but fill more of the interior with meaningful action. Background layers and secondary objects can reach toward the edges. Do not reserve a compulsory quarter of the image as empty cream. These are composition guides, not rules that justify shrinking the subject into unreadability. For a social card, use the stronger crop-safe geometry in `production.md` and simplify secondary elements.

The medium, palette temperature, edge softness, lighting, and editorial character stay consistent across posts. Density and viewpoint follow the story. Subjects and composition come from the article. Do not add a cabbage, check mark, robot, laptop, or paper card to every story merely because the reference contains one or because the topic involves software.

## Make a scene worth looking at

Use people, hands, tools, environments, and interacting objects when they express the author's actual situation. Let an action connect the subjects: taking layers apart, rescuing an old archive, passing evidence forward, or handing a decision back to a person. Build a second and third point of interest around that main action so the viewer has something to discover. Avoid an inventory of isolated icons, a row of blank cards, or the same check-mark composition for every article.

Richness is purposeful, not confetti. Every prominent object should connect to an article detail. Keep enough hierarchy that the scene reads at a glance, then rewards a closer look. Distinct colour areas, overlapping planes, paper ribbons or motion trails, and asymmetry can create movement without turning the image into a diagram. Never add invented statistics, fake screenshots, or private personal details to make it busier.

Text should earn its space; there is no target label count. Food does not need a “lunch” tag, shared material does not need an “evidence” sign, and an unhurried reader does not need a board spelling out the scheduling request. Remove the prop if it exists only to carry the word. A diagnostic packet token or a date that establishes a particular era can be useful. Exact numbers must preserve their context and caveats; measured charts still come from data. Correct any retained lettering and check its small-size legibility.

Judge the scene before judging its finish. Could a reader describe the visible action without borrowing the article's abstract nouns? Is each prominent object connected to that situation? Does anything imply a false mechanism, such as a payload inside an empty keepalive, hardware repair in a software story, or a reserved appointment when the system only notifies? Fix those problems even when the art is attractive. Supporting detail can be ordinary environmental texture; not every pencil or cup needs to encode another concept. Do not manufacture a diagram out of the whole room.

## Reuse the reference without drifting

1. Inspect the accepted reference before generation and attach the actual image. A filename in the prompt is insufficient.
2. Label it “style reference: preserve matte paper layers, grain, edges, and shallow soft shadows; enrich the palette and composition as directed; do not copy its subject.” Label any composition sketch or factual reference separately.
3. Keep the stable style paragraph below in each prompt. Vary only the article-specific scene and its composition.
4. For a local defect, edit the selected result while stating what must remain. For later posts, return to the original accepted anchor rather than repeatedly inheriting an imperfect edit.
5. Compare the new image and reference at equal displayed widths. Check material, temperature, shadows, focal hierarchy, and complexity. Inspect the mobile version before adding detail.
6. Update this guide when the author establishes a new recurring preference; keep per-image decisions transient.

A seed is not a style system. References improve repeatability but do not guarantee identical rendering across models or versions. Reuse an existing piece of artwork when exact identity matters.

## Light, dark, and other media

**Dark theme:** keep the opaque ivory art as an inset figure on the dark page. The illustration retains its internal contrast; the site's normal figure treatment supplies its edge. Do not invert it, recolour it with filters, or assume transparent dark shapes will remain visible. Inspect both themes at the actual reading width.

**Diagrams:** use the existing theme-aware Mermaid or editable SVG for mechanisms and precise arrows. Keep native labels and accessible explanations. They can share the artwork's restraint without simulating paper or adopting an opaque background.

**Screenshots and photographs:** preserve factual colours and content. Use the existing figure width, border, caption, and screenshot-grid rules. Do not apply paper style transfer to evidence or invent interface captures.

**Charts:** use actual data and native plotting. Keep axes, units, labels, and uncertainty legible; illustration style must not distort values.

**Social images:** preserve the selected paper treatment and article-specific subject, recompose the scene for the wide canvas, retaining its energy and meaningful secondary elements while removing only details that cannot read small. Keep the focal object meaningful in the central square. A headline is optional; useful short labels can remain if they survive the small preview. Compose exact or lengthy text separately when that is more reliable.

## Reusable style paragraph

> Rich editorial cut-paper illustration for a personal engineering blog. Use the attached reference for layered matte paper, natural fibre grain, softly imperfect cut edges, folds, and short soft shadows under consistent diffuse light. Build a recognisable article-specific scene around one focal action, with natural environmental detail, overlapping planes, varied scale, and a sense of movement. Warm ivory and cream ground a deliberate combination of two or three colours such as teal, coral, cobalt, sage, plum, and saffron. Give the eye several things to discover without losing the main story. Let gestures and objects carry the idea. Usually no lettering; include specified text only when its exact identity matters. No glossy clay, neon, ink outlines, watermark, fake documentary evidence, or meaningless filler.
