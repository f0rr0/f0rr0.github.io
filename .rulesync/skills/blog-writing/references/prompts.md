# Starting prompts for personal technical blogging

Use the prompt that matches the task and replace bracketed material with real input. Keep working notes separate from the article.

## Draw out missing personal material

> I want to write about [project]. Here are my rough notes: [notes]. Find the part that needs my own recollection before it can become a personal story. Ask one useful question at a time. Preserve my phrasing. Do not supply an anecdote, emotion or lesson for me. Stop asking when we have enough material to draft.

Use this only when essential material is missing. Existing drafts and detailed user accounts usually make it unnecessary.

## Calibrate the voice

> Read these authentic examples and my corrections: [examples]. Identify the sentence rhythms, transitions, humour and level of technical explanation I actually use. Give a few concrete rules with short examples. Separate my preferences from generic writing advice. Apply them to [draft] without importing the examples' events or opinions.

## Find the story

> Read [draft, notes and relevant project artifacts]. Identify what I wanted, the changes in my understanding, and the most interesting technical discovery. Reshape the article around that thread. Keep the real attempts and outcomes. Compress routine setup and wiring. Let the reader discover the mechanism through a concrete example. End where something has actually changed. Keep source checking out of the prose unless the reference helps the reader.

## Repair a defensive draft

> Revise [draft] in my voice. Remove repository-verification narration, repeated qualifications and inventories of unimplemented work. Keep a constraint when it changes how the reader understands or uses the idea. State it once in ordinary language. Preserve meaningful failed attempts, and do not upgrade a proposed feature into a completed one. Rebuild weak paragraphs around what happened and why it led to the next decision.

## Read as a stranger to the project

> Read only [article], as [intended reader]. Do not use project notes to repair missing setup. Mark the few exact passages where you become confused, stop caring, or expect a payoff that never comes. Explain the reading problem and the smallest useful repair. Preserve the author's distinctive asides and deliberate pacing. This is a reading pass, not an adversarial audit.

## Explain one thing visually

> The reader needs to understand [relationship or change]. Use [verified mechanism] to create the simplest diagram that makes it visible. Choose the diagram type to fit the question. Use real component names, short labels, and accessible title/description text. Render it and check arrow meaning, label collisions and mobile readability. Write a sentence introducing the question and a caption explaining what to notice.

For a screenshot, replace diagram generation with capture of the actual interface in the relevant state. For a conceptual illustration, describe the intended analogy, composition, palette, crop and empty space; keep exact labels and title text in code. Do not ask an image model to fabricate a screenshot or measurement.

## Package a finished article

> Read the final [article] and the site's publishing guide. Complete its title, standalone summary, useful tags, author and dates. Preserve the existing slug and original date. If the title is weak, try three distinct, honest alternatives and select the closest fit. Choose the social image and any inline visual the story needs. Check the rendered article, metadata, canonical URL, structured data, image responses, RSS and sitemap. Use the site's existing components and deployment workflow. Report the actual publication state.

## Learn from an accepted edit

> Compare my revision with the earlier draft: [pair]. Identify the recurring preference this correction expresses. Add or refine one concise rule in the writing skill, with a short example if useful. Do not treat every one-off sentence change as a permanent rule.
