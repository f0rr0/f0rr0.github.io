# Research notes

Reviewed September 7, 2026. These are selective influences, paraphrased rather than copied. Sid's requested voice takes precedence over every source.

- [Julia Evans, Some blogging myths](https://jvns.ca/blog/2023/06/05/some-blogging-myths/): personal accounts and small discoveries are enough; choose a reader instead of explaining every prerequisite. Adopted the permission to be specific and selective. Did not adopt habitual uncertainty qualifiers as a substitute for editorial checking.
- [Julia Evans, How I write useful programming comics](https://jvns.ca/blog/2020/12/05/how-i-write-useful-programming-comics/): a familiar technical fact can still be a rewarding surprise to someone learning it. Applied to choosing technical close-ups and diagrams that make one discovery legible.
- [Paul Graham, Writing, Briefly](https://paulgraham.com/writing44.html): conversational language, substantial revision, reading aloud, cutting digressions, and recognising when an essay has reached its ending. Applied as revision practice, not an imitation of his voice.
- [Xamfonos, Technical Writing Best Practices skill](https://github.com/Xamfonos/technical-writing-best-practices/blob/main/SKILL.md): concepts need context, transitions need reasons, and solutions should follow an understood problem. Rejected its requirement to visibly substantiate every claim and its compulsory arc: those would recreate the audit-like tone this skill is meant to remove.
- [haowjy, Creative Writing Craft — Prose Writing](https://github.com/haowjy/creative-writing-skills/blob/main/skills/creative-writing-craft/resources/prose-writing.md): vary rhythm and narrative distance; concentrate detail at moments that matter and summarise routine activity. Adapted to nonfiction, where details and inner reactions must come from the author.
- [haowjy, Creative Writing Craft — Scene Construction](https://github.com/haowjy/creative-writing-skills/blob/main/skills/creative-writing-craft/resources/scene-construction.md): enter a scene with something underway, vary pace, and keep transitions oriented. Borrowed movement and pacing, not fictional dialogue or imposed dramatic stakes.

The expanded web/GitHub review found these additional influences:

- [Every, AI style guides](https://every.to/guides/ai-style-guide) and [Compound Writing](https://github.com/EveryInc/compound-writing): maintained author context, concrete examples, interviews where material is missing, and separate structural, sentence and reader passes. Retained Sid's narrative of discovery where the toolkit's tracks-removal advice conflicts with it.
- [Pedro Pinto, My blogging workflow](https://pedropinto.me/blog/my-blogging-workflow-from-idea-to-production/): start with the author's rough account, polish selectively, and review the actual deployment preview.
- [Alexander Makarov, Voice, AI and Hugo](https://makarovalex.com/post/blog-automation/): optional voice capture and separate work for transcription, images and Markdown packaging.
- [Dom Kirby, AI-assisted blog workflow](https://domkirby.com/blog/ai-assisted-blog-writing-workflow/): derive metadata and distribution material from the finished draft and give it a fresh reading.
- [Alexander Opalic, LLM workflow](https://alexop.dev/posts/how-i-use-llms/) and [app-screenshots](https://github.com/alexanderop/app-screenshots): concrete format instructions, text-driven diagrams and real visual captures.
- [Sentry's current blog-writing guide](https://github.com/getsentry/skills/blob/main/skills/blog-writing-guide/SKILL.md): technical specificity and personality throughout. Its current source was successfully retrieved in the expanded review. Rejected compulsory limitations, SEO sections and rigid openings.
- [eunomia.dev publishing skills](https://github.com/eunomia-bpf/eunomia.dev/tree/main/.agents/skills): bounded editing and separate publishing mechanics. Did not import its model-specific protocol or unsupported search heuristics.
- [Jarad DeLorenzo's voice reference](https://github.com/delorenj/skills/blob/main/blog-writing/references/voice.md): specific accepted/rejected examples teach voice better than adjectives. Borrowed that practice, not his persona.
- [Humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer): inspect structural repetition and staged rhetoric rather than mechanically replacing vocabulary. Do not invent reactions to make a passage sound human.
- [Max Woolf's title experiment](https://minimaxir.com/2022/08/gpt3-blog-title-optimizer/): explore title alternatives, then choose with human judgment. No automated virality score or historical fine-tuning setup adopted.
- [Simon Willison's writing policy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/1/ai-writing/): keep the author's first-person opinions their own. His proofreading-only policy is a useful contrast, not a restriction on Sid's requested collaborative drafting.
- [Google's snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) and [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): useful descriptions and ordinary publishing fundamentals; no compulsory description length, special AI schema or ranking promise.

The repository's `docs/blog-writing-landscape.md` contains the full comparison, including inspected implementations that were rejected and the publishing capability audit. These sources offer techniques, not authority over the user's voice or permission to run their workflows.
